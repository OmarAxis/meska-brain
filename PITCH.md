# Meska Brain: the pitch

## The line

Rich countries improve schools with money. We cannot. So we tune the engine we have.

## The problem

A child misses three weeks of school in grade 3, in the term the class learns division. Nobody sees a
gap. In grade 6 the same child is failing fractions and ratios, which are built on division. The grade 6
teacher sees a child who struggles with maths. The real cause is three years old and it lives in another
teacher's notebook, or nowhere.

Every year, when a child moves to a new teacher, what the last teacher knew is lost. A teacher with fifty
learners has no time to rebuild it. Tutors, small classes and specialists solve this where there is money.
Most schools in Africa do not have that option.

## What Meska Brain does

It remembers every child's learning story across the years, and lays it over the school's own curriculum,
which already says which topic builds on which. Then it does the searching no teacher has time for:

- **Where did this difficulty start?** It walks back through the topics a struggle depends on, across years
  and across subjects, and names the earliest weak point, with the records behind it.
- **Where could this learner excel?** Results that stayed high for term after term.
- **What has helped?** Which ways of teaching were followed by better results for this learner.
- **What next?** A possible next step for the teacher.

Every statement points at the exact records it rests on. Nothing counts until a named teacher reads it and
approves it. The system never labels a child and never decides.

## Who it is for

Built for the teacher, in service of the learner, adopted by the school.

| Who | What they get |
|---|---|
| Teacher | Starts the year knowing each learner's history, strengths and where gaps began |
| Learner | Help at the real cause, and strengths that get noticed |
| Parent | A short note in their own language, approved by the teacher |
| School | Knowledge that stays when a teacher leaves; a picture of which topics the school finds hard |

## Why it is cheap

- It runs on one ordinary laptop, with no internet.
- It uses a free, open model. There is no fee per use.
- Records never leave the school.
- A new subject, grade range or country is a new curriculum file, not new software.

## What you will see in the demo

1. **Class overview.** Twelve learners followed over four years, thousands of records, four subjects.
2. **One learner's long view.** Results by subject and strand, term by term, and a knowledge graph of
   everything connected to that learner.
3. **Rewind.** Drag the term slider back. The grade 3 gap was visible years before the grade 6 struggle.
4. **Across subjects.** A learner's difficulty with maths word problems traces back to reading.
5. **The agent at work.** It reads a new quiz sheet or a teacher's note in Swahili or Arabic, records the
   evidence, and proposes a finding. Every tool call is on screen.
6. **The teacher decides.** Approve, edit or reject. The agent cannot approve anything.
7. **Try to break it.** Ask it to label a child: the server refuses, and the refusal is logged.

## Where we are now, honestly

**Built and working**

- An MCP server with ten tools, an agent that runs on a local open model, and a teacher app.
- Four subjects: mathematics, science (life science, materials, physics, earth and space), language and
  literacy, and technology, with links between subjects.
- Teacher notes in several languages, an audit log of every action, and two teacher approval gates.
- A way for teachers to describe how they teach, shown next to what has helped each learner.

**Not yet true**

- No real school has used it. All data in the demo is synthetic.
- It has been evaluated with one model and on our own data.
- The home-language notes have not been checked by native speakers.

## Where this goes

**Next for schools**

- A personal route through the same curriculum for each learner: close the earliest gap first.
- Matching learners and ways of teaching, with the teacher's own description of how they teach.
- A school-wide and district-wide view of which topics need attention.

**The same engine, beyond schools**

The core is not about children. It is three maps laid over each other: what there is to learn and in what
order, what a person has shown they can do, and what kind of support was followed by progress. Swap the
curriculum file for a job's skills map and the same engine follows an apprentice, a nurse in training or a
new employee: what they have mastered, where a difficulty really starts, and what training helped.

Because it is built on MCP, any tool a school, a training centre or an employer already uses can connect
to it, and any assistant can ask it questions, with the same rules: evidence for every claim, a log of
every action, and a named person who decides.

That is the larger idea: a cheap, open way for countries with fewer resources to know their people better
and waste less of their teaching.

## The ask

- A school willing to try it with one class for one term.
- Teachers who will tell us where it is wrong.
